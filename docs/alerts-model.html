High-ROI screen — what “the model likes” means

Tape B on the alert tapes page shows a swing only when its market clears five gates. This page is the whole model: the formulas, the constant values, the field each one reads, and what the model cannot see. It is a screen, not a forecast and not a trade signal — no order is placed anywhere, and nothing here is investment advice.

Everything is computed from two documented, unauthenticated reads. Prices and quotes come from GET /markets; resting depth comes from GET /markets/orderbooks?tickers=…; printed trades come from GET /markets/trades. No field is inferred: if a price or a size is missing, the gate fails and the line says why.

1. Contract maths (the only two formulas)

Taker fee — from the official fee schedule (footer: “Last updated and effective: July 7, 2026”):

fee = round up( M × 0.07 × C × P × (1 − P) )      M = contract multiplier (default 1)
                                                     C = contracts, P = price in dollars
maker fee uses 0.0175 in place of 0.07, with M = 0 unless the series is listed

“round up such that the fee + positionCost is rounded to a centicent” — this model rounds the fee up to the cent, which is the conservative direction, and the rounding rules are documented in Fee Rounding. Two rows of the schedule’s own table are asserted by the test suite: P=$0.50, C=100 → $1.75; P=$0.10, C=100 → $0.63.

Buying one side and holding to resolution — a winning contract pays $1.00, a losing one pays $0 (Market Settlement):

outlay  = P × C + fee
payout  = $1.00 × C                 (only if the side wins)
net ROI = (payout − outlay) / outlay
breakeven win probability = outlay / payout

Example, printed exactly as the page computes it: entry $0.2000, C = 1 → fee $0.02, outlay $0.2200, payout $1.00, net ROI 354.5 %, breakeven win probability 22.0 %. The ROI is large at low prices because the payoff is 5:1 — it is not a 354 % expected return; it is what a win pays.

2. The five gates

G1 · in-window

status is active/open/unopened and close_time is in the future. Resolved or out-of-window markets cannot qualify, which is also what keeps the page “tracking live events until they resolve”: the tape keeps a market in the watchlist until the API returns result = yes | no (or scalar), then posts a RESOLUTION line instead of a swing line.

G2 · fillable

An ask must exist on the candidate side with at least 1 contract resting (minContractsAtAsk). The API returns bids only, so the YES ask is derived from the best NO bid as $1.00 − bid and the NO ask from the best YES bid — the documented reciprocal identity in Orderbook Responses. Every line names its derivation (“derived from NO bid” or “quoted ask”). Zero depth → gate fails, and a demo book with no resting orders is common.

G3 · reversion lead

The offered ask must sit at least $0.02 (minPull) below the session VWAP — the volume-weighted average of yes_price_dollars over the public prints this browser has actually seen for that market (last 200 prints). It is not an official Kalshi VWAP field and is labelled “session VWAP” everywhere it appears; when no print has been seen the gate fails with “VWAP is blank” rather than defaulting to zero.

G4 · payoff

Fee-adjusted net ROI ≥ 0.10 (minRoi, i.e. at least 10 %) at the current ask, computed with the formulas above. This is what keeps the right-hand tape about payoff rather than about noise: a $0.94 ask cannot clear it, a $0.14 ask can.

G5 · swing trigger

A move of ≥ $0.10 in either direction between two consecutive reads — the brief’s threshold, editable on the page. Deltas are compared on the market’s mark: the traded last price when one exists, otherwise the book mid. At most one alert per market per direction per 60 s (cooldownMs) so a single jump cannot flood the tape.

3. Score (ranking only, never a threshold)

depthWeight = 0.5 + 0.5 × min(1, contractsAtAsk / 100)
pullWeight  = 1 + min(1, max(0, sessionVWAP − ask) / 0.20)
score       = net ROI × depthWeight × pullWeight      (only when all five gates pass)

The score orders Tape B and is shown on each line so any reader can redo it from the printed inputs. Depth caps at 100 contracts because deeper books are more likely to absorb a real order; the reversion bonus caps at 1.0 so an extreme gap cannot dominate the payoff.

4. What the model cannot see (deliberately excluded)

  • No news feed. The API returns prices only — there is no official field that says why a market moved (candlesticks). Each line links a news search for the market title as a lead; a search result is never presented as a cause.
  • No order book beyond the visible top levels, no queue position, no other participants’ resting sizes.
  • No settlement probability from the underlying source (AAA, ESPN, a court filing…). G3 compares the market to its own traded history, not to the truth.
  • No fees beyond the trade fee. The docs state settlement fees are zero for simple yes/no determinations but may apply to sub-cent scalar settlement, and the actual payout is rounded to whole cents (Market Settlement).
  • No series fee multipliers. A handful of series carry non-standard maker/taker multipliers (for example KXAAAGASM charges maker fees at M = 1); the screen applies the general table with M = 1 and says so. Where the multiplier is not 1 the printed ROI is optimistic on the maker side only.
  • No streaming. A WebSocket subscription would be lower latency, but its handshake requires a signed, authenticated connection that a browser page cannot make without a private key (Quick Start: WebSockets) — and a public static site must never hold one.
  • Not a fill guarantee. GET /markets responses are cached “for a short time”, so the ask quoted on a line may already be gone; lines are marked with the request that produced them.

6. Verification of this spec

  • Maths: node tests/alerts.test.mjs asserts the two fee-schedule table rows, the payout/ROI identity, both swing directions, the derived-ask identity, and that a gate failure blocks Tape B.
  • Behaviour: the same harness boots alerts.html in jsdom against mocked official responses and checks that a swing line, its source links, a RESOLUTION line and a model pick render as specified.
  • Not verified: the screen has no live win-rate claim. The repo publishes no performance figure for these gates, because a demo venue with mock funds and a screen this small would make any such number meaningless.
