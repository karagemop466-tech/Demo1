The model behind Feed B

Feed B is a screen, not a forecast. Five terms, each with a fixed weight, produce a score out of 100. A row appears in Feed B only when the score clears the bar and both ROI figures clear theirs. Everything is printed below so any row can be recomputed by hand, and the screen's own hit rate is measured against the official trade tape rather than asserted.

Why a screen and not "alpha". The demo venue is a test exchange whose books are thin and whose prices are not real-money prices. A model that claimed predictive edge here would be claiming something the data cannot support. What the screen does claim is narrower and checkable: after a ≥ $0.10 move, it prefers markets where entering is cheap in spread, deep enough to fill, and far enough from the $1 cap to pay for the fee.

The score

Five terms, fixed weights, no hidden inputs

score = Σ points, 0 – 100
TermWeightHow the points are computedWhy it is there
Jump size30min(1, |move| / $0.30) × 30A bigger printed move is a bigger signal that something changed.
Spread25max(0, 1 − spread / 10¢) × 25A wide spread eats the entire move on entry and exit.
Depth at touch20min(1, contracts at touch / 200) × 20A price you cannot fill is not a price.
Room to $115min(1, 1 − entry ask) × 15Binary contracts pay at most $1, so a 90¢ entry cannot pay much.
Freshness10max(0, 1 − age / 15m) × 10A move from an hour ago is history, not an alert.

The selection bar

Score ≥ 55
Sum of the five terms above.
Net ROI ≥ 25%
((1 − ask) − feePerContract) / (ask + feePerContract) — the return if the position wins, after the official taker fee. This is the term that keeps 5¢ contracts with a 2¢ fee out of the feed.
Entry ask ≤ $0.85
Above 85¢, a win pays less than 18¢ per contract before fees, so the trade cannot clear the ROI bar anyway.
Also shown: max ROI
1 / ask − 1 — the conventional prediction-market payout figure, fee-free. A 20¢ contract shows +400%. It is displayed for context but the screen filters on net ROI, because fees are real.
